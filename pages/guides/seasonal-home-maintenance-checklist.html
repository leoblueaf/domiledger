Home maintenance checklist by season

Most expensive home repairs start as small, cheap ones that nobody noticed. A seasonal rhythm catches them early without turning every weekend into a project.

Use this as a starting point, not a rulebook. Your climate, the age of the house, and the equipment you actually have matter more than any generic list. Where your manufacturer's instructions differ, follow the manufacturer.

Every month

  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Press the test button; replace batteries as needed.
  • Check the HVAC filter. Many filters need replacing every 1 to 3 months, sooner with pets or during heavy use.
  • Test GFCI outlets in kitchens, bathrooms, garages and outdoors (press Test, then Reset).
  • Look under sinks for drips, stains or soft cabinet floors.

Spring

  • Clean gutters and downspouts after trees finish dropping seeds and blossoms, and make sure water drains away from the foundation.
  • Schedule cooling service before the first hot week, when HVAC companies get busy.
  • Walk the exterior. Look at the roof from the ground for missing or lifted shingles, and check siding, trim and caulk around windows and doors.
  • Test the sump pump before the rainy season by pouring water into the pit until it switches on.
  • Check outdoor faucets and hoses for freeze damage from winter.
  • Service irrigation and check sprinkler heads if you have them.

Summer

  • Clean the dryer vent. Lint buildup is a leading cause of dryer fires; clean the duct at least once a year, not just the lint trap.
  • Keep the outdoor AC unit clear of leaves, grass clippings and plants.
  • Inspect decks and railings for loose boards, popped fasteners and wobbly rails.
  • Check the garage door's auto-reverse by placing an object in its path.
  • Clean the refrigerator coils and replace the water filter if yours has one.

Fall

  • Schedule heating service before the first cold night.
  • Clean gutters again once the leaves are down.
  • Shut off and drain outdoor faucets in climates that freeze, and disconnect hoses.
  • Have the chimney inspected before the first fire if you use a fireplace or wood stove.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and exterior penetrations to cut drafts.
  • Flush the water heater if the manufacturer recommends it, to reduce sediment.

Winter

  • Know where your main water shutoff is and make sure it turns. A burst pipe is the wrong time to look for it.
  • Watch for ice dams and icicles along the roof edge, a sign of heat escaping into the attic.
  • Keep vents clear of snow, including furnace and water heater exhausts.
  • Check fire extinguishers: the gauge should be in the green and the pin intact.
  • Test your emergency plan: flashlights, generator (outdoors only, away from windows), and escape routes.

Once a year, whenever it fits

  • Inspect the attic for leaks, pests and insulation gaps.
  • Check caulk and grout in showers and tubs.
  • Review appliance warranties and the ages of major systems.
  • Replace smoke alarms that are 10 years old or older, and CO alarms at the age the manufacturer specifies.
Safety first. Anything involving gas, electrical panels, roofs or ladders at height is often better left to a licensed professional. A checklist tells you what to look at; it doesn't mean you have to do the repair yourself.

Write it down

A checklist tells you what to do. A record tells you what you did: the date, who did it, what it cost, and what they found. That history is what helps you spot patterns (the AC that needs a recharge every summer) and what a future buyer or insurer will want to see.
