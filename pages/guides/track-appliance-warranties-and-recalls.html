How to track appliance warranties and product recalls

The day a dishwasher leaks is the worst day to find out you don't know its model number, when you bought it, or whether it's still under warranty.

Finding the data plate

  • Dishwasher: the edge of the door or the inside of the tub frame.
  • Refrigerator: inside the fresh-food compartment, on a side wall or ceiling.
  • Washer and dryer: inside the door or lid, or on the back panel.
  • Range or oven: behind the storage drawer or around the oven door frame.
  • Furnace, AC and water heater: a label on the cabinet or tank; outdoor AC units have one on the side panel.

Take a clear photo of each plate. It takes a few seconds and saves you from lying on the kitchen floor with a flashlight later.

Warranties: what to know

  • Manufacturer warranties on major appliances are often around one year for the whole unit, sometimes longer for specific parts such as compressors or motors. Read the actual terms; they vary.
  • Registration still helps. In the U.S., the FTC says a warranty generally can't require you to mail in a registration card to be covered, but registering with the manufacturer means they can contact you about a recall.
  • Extended plans from retailers are separate contracts. Record who issued them and how to file a claim.
  • Set reminders ahead of the end date. If something is acting up, you want time to book a service call while it's still covered.

Checking for product recalls

In the U.S., the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) publishes recalls for household products, including many appliances. You can search recalls at cpsc.gov/Recalls and report product problems at SaferProducts.gov.

  • Search by brand and the product type (for example "dishwasher").
  • Compare the exact model number and any listed serial or date ranges with your data plate. A brand match alone doesn't mean your unit is affected.
  • Follow the remedy in the notice (repair, replacement or refund) and contact the manufacturer directly.
  • Recalls are announced over time, so an appliance that was fine last year may not be fine this year. Check periodically.
If a recall involves a fire, shock or gas hazard, stop using the product as the notice instructs until the remedy is done.
